# Cheap Travels India — The Story Behind the Name

A short, reusable narrative for the homepage, about page, marketing decks, investor intros, partner pitches, and customer-service onboarding. Three lengths so you never have to rewrite.

---

## Tagline (use on logo lockups, business cards, social bio)

> **Sasti booking, pakka ticket.**
> *(Fair fare. Full journey.)*

Use the Hindi line in North-India campaigns, the English line in metros + investor decks. Together on the website. Either alone on Instagram.

> That bird is our quiet hero. Because the traveller in our country deserves the same — to go far without paying for a feast.

>
> The name *"Cheap"* isn't an apology. It's a promise: **fair fare, full journey.**

## Why the parrot? — the reasoning (use this in interviews / pitches)

Use these talking points when someone asks "why a parrot?" or "isn't 'cheap' a weak brand name?":

1. **The parrot is an Indian icon of free movement.** From folk tales (*tota-maina*) to Punjabi songs to children's books, the parrot represents agency and travel without weight. It belongs to *every* Indian state — North, South, East, North-East. No region owns it. No religion owns it.
2. **Parrots travel far on small meals.** That's the entire business model in one image: maximum journey from minimum money. The customer sees the parrot and intuitively gets the value proposition.
3. **"Cheap" + parrot reframes the word.** Without the parrot, "Cheap" reads as low-quality. With the parrot, "Cheap" reads as *clever, efficient, full-of-life*. The mascot does the brand-rehab work for free.
4. **Mascots scale across the journey.** We have 9 mascot variants — for bookings, cabs, pilgrimage, support, safety, holidays. The same character carries the brand across every product line you'll launch.
5. **It's emotionally resonant in a transactional category.** RedBus is a search box. AbhiBus is a search box. MakeMyTrip is a search box. Cheap Travels is a character with a story. In a market this commoditised, story is the moat.

## How to talk about "Cheap" with concerned customers / partners

If a customer or partner pushes back ("the word *cheap* feels low-end"), use this rebuttal verbatim:

> *"We hear you. In English, 'cheap' can sound like 'low quality'. We chose it deliberately — because in real Indian life, 'सस्ता' (sasta) is what people search for. Sasta doesn't mean poor quality. It means the right price for the value. Our buses are the same Volvos and AC sleepers everyone else sells. The fare is just fairer. The promise is in the name."*

---

## Three never-do rules around the brand

1. **Never apologise for the name** in any official copy. The name is a positioning, not a placeholder.
2. **Never put "Cheap" beside images of unsafe / dilapidated buses** — even ironically. Always pair "Cheap" with premium-looking AC sleepers, Volvos, branded coaches.
3. **Never translate the brand name to Hindi** on the logo. *"Cheap Travels India"* stays in English script. (Reason: budget-travel English brand names test better for trust in India — see Flipkart, Zomato, Swiggy, Ola — they all kept English wordmarks even though they target Hindi-belt customers.)
